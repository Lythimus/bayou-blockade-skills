# Louisiana legal hooks — status and traps

Check every authority here before citing it. Each entry carries its current status and the
specific way it can be misused. **Never characterize a case from memory** — one case in this
file reads as favorable and is not, and it has already been miscited in a filed comment.

Add new authorities here as they are verified, with status and date.

---

## Constitutional and public-trust

### La. Const. art. IX, § 1 — the public trust duty
**Status: live, foundational.** Natural resources "shall be protected, conserved, and
replenished insofar as possible, consistent with the health, safety, and welfare of the
people." The source of the whole IT-factors framework. Safe to cite directly.

### *Save Ourselves, Inc. v. Louisiana Environmental Control Commission*, 452 So.2d 1152 (La. 1984)
**Status: live, controlling.** Establishes the agency's public-trustee obligation and the
"IT factors" — before issuing, the agency must determine:

1. whether potential and real adverse environmental effects have been **avoided to the maximum
   extent possible**;
2. whether a **cost-benefit analysis** shows social and economic benefits outweigh
   environmental costs;
3. whether **alternative projects, alternative sites, or mitigating measures** offering more
   protection were considered.

**Where it bites hardest:** factor 3. A record with zero alternatives analysis fails it
factually, with no legal argument required. Factor 2 is nearly as good where the only stated
justification is the applicant's own administrative convenience and no public benefit is
asserted anywhere. When the applicant does claim jobs or investment, its own incentive filings
put a number on that claim and show its track record against earlier promises. Pull them with
`bayou:itep-lookup` (promised against reported jobs, BCI compliance codes and votes).

### *RISE St. James v. LDEQ* (Formosa) — ⚠️ **READ THIS BEFORE CITING**
**Verified against the opinion text, 2026-10-16 (`bayou:la-court-opinions`):** RISE St. James v.
La. Dep't of Envtl. Quality, 2023-0578 (La. App. 1 Cir. 1/19/24). Reporter cite 383 So.3d 956
appears in a filed Formosa comment (`ej-comment-style/references/excerpts.md`, pin 980 for slip
p. 32) but is not on CourtListener, so it is unverified; slip p. 32 does discuss SILs. The decree reads "JUDGMENT
REVERSED; PERMITS REINSTATED; JUDGMENT RENDERED." Rehearing applications by LDEQ, Beverly
Alexander and the RISE plaintiffs were acted on Feb. 15, 2024 (two read "DENIED"; the third
stamp is illegible in the scan). LASC writ history: the plaintiffs applied for writs on March 18, 2024 (Earthjustice's filed
application), and a Liskow & Lewis client note says "On September 4, 2024, the Supreme Court
summarily denied these petitions." **Docket number and the LASC action itself not yet seen.**
Confirm on lasc.org before writing "writ denied, 2024-____ (La. 9/4/24)". CourtListener has no
record of the denial, which is the writ-denial gap `bayou:la-court-opinions` warns about. *Alexander v. St. James Parish*, 2025-C-00866 (La. 3/6/26), shares parties but is a parish
land-use case, not history of this appeal.

**Do not describe this as a ruling against Formosa. It reinstated Formosa's permits.**

- **19th JDC, September 14, 2022** — vacated all of Formosa's air permits on cumulative-impact
  and environmental-justice grounds. Widely reported as a landmark.
- **La. 1st Cir., January 19, 2024 — REVERSED IN ITS ENTIRETY.** The panel majority reinstated
  every permit. This is the operative decision.

**What you can accurately cite from the First Circuit opinion:** it held the *Save Ourselves*
directives "broad enough to include an analysis of environmental justice, as defined by the EPA"
(Op. at 46) — so EJ
analysis being part of the Louisiana public trust duty is First Circuit law.

**The trap, and it is a real one:** the same opinion endorses the sequence where, **upon
finding no adverse impact, the agency need not analyze disparate impact.** That is the
applicant's standard defense, and this case supplies it (Op. at 51: DEQ's "determination that
there were no 'adverse impacts' made it unnecessary to reach the issue of 'disparate impact'"). Citing the case for EJ without
addressing this hands the agency its own answer.

**How to use it correctly:** cite it for the EJ holding, then **distinguish it on the
predicate.** The escape hatch requires a defensible no-adverse-impact finding first. Where the
record's impact finding is conclusory — one sentence, no receptor, no concentration, no
location, or made over an unresolved internal technical objection — the agency cannot take the
benefit of a sequence whose first step it never completed. Say that explicitly.

## Statutory

### La. R.S. 30:2018 — Environmental Assessment Statement
- **§ 2018(E)(2)** exempts "an application for a minor source of air emissions" from § 2018
  outright. **Verified against primary text at `legis.la.gov`.** For a minor source, the
  absence of a formal EAS from the record is **lawful**. Do not argue an EAS was required —
  that argument is unavailable and making it costs credibility on everything else.
- **§ 2018(H)** is the savings clause and names *Save Ourselves* explicitly: nothing in the
  section relieves applicants or the department of the Article IX public-trustee requirements.

**The move this supports** — used successfully in the EMPCo filing: concede the exemption
plainly, then assert the constitutional duty through (H). The statutory exemption removes the
filing formality and leaves the constitutional obligation fully intact. Conceding the point
that cuts against you buys the rest.

### La. R.S. 30:2050.21 — appeal by an aggrieved person
**The standing provision.** An aggrieved person may appeal a final permit action **only to the
19th Judicial District Court**, by petition filed **within 30 days** after notice of the action
is given.

Consequences for drafting: state standing in the letter; request written notice of the final
decision so the 30-day clock starts cleanly; do not let the comment period close without every
colorable issue raised, because unraised issues are generally waived.

### La. R.S. 30:209(4)(e) — state CO2 storage agreements need a parish hearing
**Verified against primary text at `legis.la.gov` (2026-10-17).** The State Mineral and Energy
Board may enter an operating agreement for CO2 (or hydrocarbon) storage beneath state land only
"upon a two-thirds vote of the members", after notice to the affected parish's governing
authority, and after "a public hearing conducted by a hearing officer ... in each affected
parish pursuant to R.S. 30:6". Use it to demand the hearing in the right parish when an AoR
crosses a parish line (`bayou:la-state-lands ccs` shows each agreement's parish split). Count
the votes against the two-thirds requirement.

### La. R.S. 41:1701 — state water bottoms are public-trust lands
**Verified against primary text (2026-10-17).** The beds of navigable waters and the shores of
bays, the Gulf and navigable lakes belong to the state and "shall be protected, administered,
and conserved". This pairs with Art. IX § 1 for pore-space agreements under state lakes.
`bayou:la-state-lands at` shows whether the State Land Office claims a given bottom.

### La. R.S. 30:2024 — do not confuse with the above
Pertains **exclusively to the permit applicant's** own request for a hearing within 30 days.
It has no bearing on the appeal window available to an aggrieved member of the public, which
runs under § 2050.21 from when notice of the action was given.

## Public trusts and bond financing (`postures/financing.md`)

⚠️ **La. R.S. 30:2050.21 does not apply to any of these.** It is LDEQ's permit-appeal statute.
Nothing in a TEFRA hearing, a State Bond Commission approval, or a public-trust bond issue is a
"final permit action," and citing it to a board advised by bond counsel costs the whole letter.

### La. R.S. 9:2341 — public trusts authorized; purposes
**Status: live. Verified against the text at legis.la.gov (d=107069), 2026-09-24.** This is the
Public Trust Act. §2341(A) authorizes express trusts to issue obligations "for the furtherance
and accomplishment of any authorized public function or purpose" of the state or a governmental
unit. §2341(B)(1) lists those purposes very broadly. The list includes solid-waste disposal and
industrial development.

**The trap:** the definition is broad enough that arguing "this project is not a public purpose"
rarely wins. The productive move is to ask what the borrower has actually filed to demonstrate
benefit.

### La. R.S. 9:2347(G)–(H) — the clock for public-trust bonds, and the SBC requirement
**Status: live. Verified verbatim at legis.la.gov (d=107075), 2026-09-11 and 2026-09-24.**
- **(G):** bonds carrying the statutory recital "shall be incontestable for any cause
  whatsoever **after thirty days from the date of publication of the notice of sale** of the
  bonds."
- **(H):** "All bond issues of a public trust shall be submitted to and approved by the State
  Bond Commission prior to the issuance and delivery of said bonds." The provision has no
  exception for conduit or private-activity bonds. The notice of sale is published "After
  approval by the State Bond Commission … and at least seven days prior to the sale." For
  thirty days after that publication, "any person or persons in interest shall have the right
  to contest the legality" of the proceedings and the bonds.

**The trap:** the clock runs from the **notice of sale**, not from the authorizing resolution
and not from the TEFRA hearing. A draft of the LPFA letter asked to be notified of the wrong
event. Always ask for written notice of the notice-of-sale publication date.

### La. R.S. 13:5121 et seq. — Bond Validation Act (suits to determine validity)
**Status: live. Verified at legis.la.gov (d=77948–77957), 2026-09-24.** This is the
**procedural vehicle** for a validity suit: a motion for judgment, two newspaper publications,
and a hearing 10–13 days after the second publication (§5125). §5121(2) expressly includes
"public trusts."

**The trap:** it supplies **no limitations period of its own**. §5122 preserves "any
peremptive or prescriptive period … established pursuant to the constitution and statutes of
this state." For public-trust bonds, the period is 9:2347(G) above. The Gemini-drafted
`LPFA Bond Approval Analysis.md` states "30 days from publication of the authorizing
resolution" under 13:5121. **That is wrong on both the statute and the trigger.**

### *Louisiana Public Facilities Authority v. Foster*, No. 2001-CA-0009 (La. 9/18/01)
**Status: holding verified (FindLaw text), 2026-09-24. So.2d cite still unconfirmed (checked
2026-10-16):** CourtListener lists 795 So.2d 288 (and 2001 WL 1085064) for docket 2001-CA-0009,
decided 9/18/01, and does not list 789 So.2d 1196. But it also assigns 795 So.2d 288 to *In re
Hardy*, 2001-OB-2428 (La. 9/13/01), so one of the two records is wrong. Cite by docket number
and date until someone checks the first page in the reporter.

Held: "the LPFA is a public entity, subject to legislative regulation," not a private citizen
protected by the contract clause (La. Const. art. I, § 23). The court also noted the legislature
subjected it to "public records, open meetings, and public bids, as well as to legislative
audit."

**Use:** LPFA is a public trust subject to open-meetings, public-records, and audit law. That
supports requests to put disclosures in the public record. **Do not** use it to call LPFA a
state agency. It is a public trust, and saying otherwise invites a correction.

### *Board of Directors of the Industrial Development Board of the City of Gonzales v. All Taxpayers* (Cabela's), 2005-2298 (La. 9/6/06), 938 So.2d 11
**Status: verified via search of the opinion text, 2026-09-24. Treat it as adverse.** It sets
the test for whether a transfer of public funds or property is gratuitous under La. Const. art.
VII, § 14(A). All three prongs must be met:
1. The expenditure serves a public purpose within the entity's authority.
2. Taken as a whole, the expenditure is not gratuitous.
3. The entity has "a demonstrable, objective, and reasonable expectation of receiving at least
   equivalent value."

The court upheld tax-increment bonds for a private retail store.

**The trap:** this case is why "no public purpose" arguments against industrial financing lose.
**The usable part is prong (iii).** Where public funds or property actually move, as with a
PILOT lease-back, a cooperative endeavor agreement, or incentives (`bayou:itep-lookup`), ask the public body to put
its *demonstrable, objective* expectation of equivalent value in the record. Frame it as a
disclosure request, not as a claim that the test fails. A pure conduit bond secured only by the
borrower may not involve public funds at all. Check that before invoking art. VII, § 14.

### Louisiana Legislative Auditor, *Louisiana Public Facilities Authority* performance audit (March 2000)
**Status: a real document, read in full for the LPFA letter, and 26 years old.** No more recent
performance audit repeating its findings has been found. Find it and any later report with
`bayou:la-legislative-auditor`; its entry there shows a 2005 posting date, not March 2000. Its findings:
- LPFA "does not require borrowers to demonstrate that the bond issue benefits the State."
- 60% of reviewed project-bond borrowers would or probably would have done the project anyway.
- The out-of-state-borrower benefit observation.

**Always frame these as a question about current practice**, e.g. "What does the Authority
require today?" Never assert that the deficiency is current.

## Local land use (`postures/land-use.md`)

⚠️ **R.S. 30:2050.21 and R.S. 49:964 do not apply to a local land-use decision.** The first is LDEQ's
permit appeal; the second is state-agency review.

Statutes below were read at legis.la.gov on 2026-11-03. On the same date, each case below other than
*Alexander* was found by reporter cite with `verify-cite`, and its quoted language was checked against
CourtListener's opinion text. Reporter pin pages weren't checked. `verify-cite` lists only LASC opinions
naming the parties, which misses one-line writ denials, so read the writ history before citing an
appellate case as final.

### *Alexander v. St. James Parish*, 2025-C-00866 c/w 2025-C-00868 (La. 3/6/26) — ⚠️ **READ THIS BEFORE CITING**
**Status: read in full from lasc.org (`www.lasc.org/opinions/2026/25-0866.C.OPN.pdf`), 2026-11-03. Rehearing
history and reporter cite not checked. Adverse to challengers.** The court of appeal had reversed a parish's
land-use approval (24-557, La. App. 5 Cir. 5/14/25, 415 So.3d 437). The Supreme Court **vacated that ruling and
reinstated the district court's**. Guidry, J., dissented.

**Do not cite the Fifth Circuit opinion as a win.**

Holds (slip op. 16–20): the arbitrary-and-capricious standard governs all local land-use decisions, whether
reviewing a regulation or its application; "[t]here should be no de novo review"; the parish's interpretation of
its own plan gets deference; "should" in that plan was permissive. Fn. 13: an ambiguous land-use ordinance is
construed for the least restricted use (*City of New Orleans v. Elms*).

**The trap:** "the parish failed to follow its own ordinance" lost here, because the court treated it as a
disagreement over interpretation. The only opinion saying that a parish "acts outside of the confines of its
power when it fails to follow its own ordinance" is the dissent.

**How to use it:** cite it for the standard you must meet. Keep Tier 1 to steps that were or weren't taken and to
counts of days, which call for no interpretation. Whether *Alexander* changes the "de novo proceeding" practice
of hearing new evidence in a rezoning challenge is not addressed.

### *Palermo Land Co. v. Planning Comm'n of Calcasieu Parish*, 561 So.2d 482 (La. 1990); *King v. Caddo Parish Comm'n*, 97-1873 (La. 10/20/98), 719 So.2d 410; *Toups v. City of Shreveport*, 10-1559 (La. 3/15/11), 60 So.3d 1215
**Status: found by `verify-cite`; quoted language confirmed in CourtListener's text, 2026-11-03.** Zoning is legislative;
"Courts will not and cannot substitute their judgment" (*Palermo*); "room for two opinions" (*King*). *Toups* applies the
standard to a council's denial of a special exception use. **The trap:** all three call a challenge "a de novo proceeding", and
*Alexander* says that doesn't mean de novo review.

### R.S. 33:4780.40–.50 — parish zoning
Excludes Rapides, Jefferson, Sabine and Webster (.40). Review grounds (.40). In accordance with a comprehensive plan (.42,
.43). Council hearing: weekly in three different weeks, 15 days, certified mail if ten parcels or fewer (.43). Protest:
majority of the members, **not two-thirds** (.44). Commission hearing and final report before council action (.45). Board
of adjustment (.46). Petition within 30 days of filing in the board's office; "any opponent before the board" is a party
(.47). Local bodies can't override state environmental regulation or permitted activity (.49). **The trap:** .50(C) on home
rule parishes. Check the charter before quoting a count.

### R.S. 33:4721–4729 — municipal zoning
Differs from the parish rules: regular mail (4724(C)); protest vote is a majority "present and voting" (4725); board majority
present and voting (4727(D)(2)). Do not cite it for parish land outside a municipality (*Coogan*).

### R.S. 33:101.1 — plat approval is legislative; review confined to the record
**Amended by Acts 2025, No. 277, eff. June 11, 2025.** Review "shall be confined to the record established before the governing
authority, the planning commission, or planning administrator whose decision is being appealed." **The trap:** the digest of the
bill as introduced is not the enacted text; the record language is confirmed from the current statute page. Sets no deadline, and
doesn't say who may sue.

### R.S. 33:109, 33:112, 33:113 — plan and plats
§ 109(A): mandatory referral of public ways and utilities, two-thirds override. § 109(B): "shall consider" an adopted plan. **The
trap:** that is a duty to consider, and a note points to Acts 2004, No. 859, § 2 on applicability, which hasn't been read. § 113:
60 days or deemed approved; a hearing before action; ground of disapproval on the record; five-day notices.

### R.S. 42:13, 42:14(D), 42:19, 42:24–.26 — Open Meetings
Planning and zoning commissions are public bodies (42:13(A)(3)). Comment before a vote (42:14(D)). 24-hour agenda, reasonable
specificity, unanimous vote to add an item (42:19(A)(1)(b)). Voidable, suit within 60 days (42:24). Any person may sue (42:25(C));
fees to a prevailing plaintiff (42:26(C)).

### *Tassin v. City of Marksville*, 411 So.2d 1240 (La. App. 3 Cir. 1982)
"Failure to give the required notice is fatal to the rezoning ordinance despite the fact that some property owners had actual notice."
The notice was posted signs required by the local ordinance. Earlier non-compliance was no waiver. **The trap:** it predates *Alexander*.
Use it for a step not taken, not for a disputed reading.

### *Coogan v. Parish of Jefferson*, 390 So.2d 515 (La. 1980)
Per curiam: the 30-day period in R.S. 33:4727 didn't apply to a suit over a parish council's refusal to rezone, with no municipality or
board of adjustment involved. **The trap:** "reasonable time" and "[s]ix months is beyond a reasonable time" are from Justice Lemmon's
**dissent**. It predates the parish subpart (Acts 1993, No. 201). It doesn't set a deadline for rezoning challenges.

### *Cupit v. City of New Orleans*, 120 So.3d 862 (La. App. 4 Cir. 2013)
Neighboring owners are "aggrieved persons" under R.S. 33:4727(E)(1) for a board of zoning adjustments appeal. Municipal statute and board
appeal only.

### *1900 Highway 190, L.L.C. v. City of Slidell*, 196 So.3d 693 (La. App. 1 Cir. 2016)
**The traps:** the challenge to the ordinance failed as untimely under R.S. 33:175, for annexation, even though the ordinance also rezoned, so
don't cite it as a rezoning deadline. The majority's no-right-of-action ruling on competitive harm, relying on *Lauer v. City of Kenner*, 536 So.2d
767, disposed of the inverse condemnation claim. McDonald, J., agreeing with reasons, is the one who applies it to contesting a zoning ordinance. *Lauer* is the closer authority for that.

### *Shaw v. Jefferson Parish*, 186 So.3d 1181 (La. App. 5 Cir. 2016)
A zoning challenge "is not an appeal from a decision of a lower tribunal wherein the reviewing court scrutinizes the record below". **The trap:**
it predates *Alexander*; don't state it as current law without saying so.

## Regulatory (LAC Title 33)

- **LAC 33:I.Chapter 18 — expedited permitting.** Where a large or complex application was run
  on the expedited track, ask for the designation to be rescinded and the application processed
  on the standard track commensurate with its scale.
- **LAC 33:III.531.A.1 — state-only public notice.** Watch for a notice claiming state-only
  status over a permit containing conditions marked "(Federally Enforceable)." That mismatch is
  a notice defect.
- **LAC 33:III.319 — Regulatory Permit for Flaring of Materials Other than Natural Gas.**
  Requires, per event and in advance: physical location (§ 319.G.1.e), dates and expected
  duration (§ 319.G.1.f), estimated criteria and toxic emissions (§ 319.G.1.g), separate
  notification per event (§ 319.G.3); authorization lapses 60 days after completeness
  (§ 319.D); events over 10 days need separate approval (§ 319.E); volume monitored by flow
  meter with an actual-emissions report within 30 days (§ 319.H). **No exit-velocity limit
  exists in § 319** — verified against primary text; do not assert one. In force unchanged
  since 2018.
- **LAC 33:III.513 — relocation and temporary sources.** § 513.C.1 authorizes relocation only
  for a facility "presently operating under a certificate of approval"; § 513.C.2 makes the
  test comparative against an approved baseline (plot plan, property-line distances, prior
  dispersion estimate). § 513.B.3.b (Temporary Sources) requires notice **at least 10 days in
  advance of each change in location.** Where a permit cites § 513.C with no baseline anywhere
  in the record, both readings are adverse — say so.
- **LAC 33:VII.709.5 — solid waste land use.** Requires a description of total existing land
  use within 3 miles. A missing one goes to technical completeness.

## Case law — other

### *In re Browning-Ferris Industries Petit Bois Landfill*, 93-2050 (La. App. 1 Cir. 6/23/95), 657 So.2d 633
**Status: read in CourtListener's text, 2026-11-03, including p. 637.** Writs denied in Nos. 95-C-2127 and 95-C-2155 (La. 11/27/95), 663 So.2d 742; both actions read. `verify-cite` lists later Louisiana citing opinions as 0, and CourtListener lacks 1st, 3rd and 5th Cir. opinions after 2018, so later treatment isn't established.

**Holds:** the Secretary acted arbitrarily and capriciously in granting a landfill permit where the alternative-sites study couldn't support an informed determination; reversed and remanded (pp. 639–40). The court "pretermit[ted]" every other issue. It is "inherently unreasonable ... to limit consideration of alternative sites to arbitrary geographical boundaries where the potential benefits and risks of the proposed facility will impact a multi-parish, if not a multi-state region" (p. 639). Where "only one of the evaluated sites within the optimum radius merits favorable consideration, the search radius should be expanded" (p. 639).

**Useful facts:** sixteen of the seventeen sites were carried over from a study for another facility, and "[t]he only new site to be considered was the location proposed" (p. 638). Eight fell out on a minimum-size cut, and the rest were scored pass/fail, which a DEQ witness called "very crude" and the applicant's consultant conceded was worse than a numerical ranking (pp. 638–39). P. 637 places the burden of proof on the applicant and lists the five IT questions, quoting *Blackett*.

**The trap:** it does **not** hold that an analysis performed to justify a site the applicant already owns fails the IT test. "Already owns" isn't in the opinion, and ownership isn't discussed as a ground. Argue that point from the facts above, or from *Save Ourselves* IT factor 3, and label it as argument. The court also said it knew of "no precise means for determining the sufficiency of an alternate sites analysis", and that acceptable sites within a parish may not require a wider search (p. 639). It dates from 1995, so check the current DEQ rules.

## Federal, as applied through state permits

- **40 C.F.R. § 60.18** — flare requirements. § 60.18(c)(3) net heating value, **§ 60.18(c)(4)
  exit velocity.** The two work together: a 300 Btu/scf heat content licenses a maximum
  velocity on the order of 60 ft/sec. A permit imposing the heat-content criterion while
  omitting the velocity criterion has taken half the federal test.
- **The duty to respond to significant comments.** An agency must consider and respond to
  significant comments; failure is grounds to set a decision aside as arbitrary and capricious.
  The specificity requirement exists to confirm the agency had notice of the challenge and a
  chance to address its substance. This is the doctrinal reason specificity outperforms volume.
- **40 C.F.R. Part 60 Subpart A (§ 60.11)** — visible emissions and opacity, applicable to
  flares; relevant where dark or opaque smoke was observed and documented.
