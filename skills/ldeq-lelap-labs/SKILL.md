# LDEQ LELAP laboratory accreditation

LAC 33:I.4501.A.2: "Laboratory data generated by commercial environmental laboratories that are not accredited under this Subpart shall not be accepted by the department." When a permit file relies on data from a lab that was not accredited for that analysis on the sample date, that is a procedural defect you can raise in a comment, and it is cheap to check. Three public sources answer the question, with no login and no CAPTCHA:

- **Current scopes.** [Laboratory Scopes of Accreditation](https://internet.deq.louisiana.gov/portal/divisions/lelap/laboratory-scopes-of-accreditation) has one row per lab × matrix × analyte × method, about 72,000 rows in all. Each row gives the LELAP lab ID, AI number, lab state and Accreditation Type. The page is a DNN OpenWebStudio module. The script scrapes its module config and dropdown values each run, then queries the AJAX filter endpoint for only the lab or method asked about.
- **Lab directory.** [Accredited Laboratories](https://internet.deq.louisiana.gov/portal/divisions/lelap/accredited-laboratories) lists 166 sites by AI number. The page also shows contact names, phones and emails. The script keeps only the AI, name, physical-location state and mailing city.
- **History.** The live tables have no dates and no history. LDEQ files each lab's annual certificate and scope, scope amendments, withdrawals and suspension notices in **EDMS under the lab's own AI number**, with media "LELAP". The anonymous EDMS search indexes them. For ARS Analytical (AI 119652), EDMS holds 345 LELAP documents from 2005 to 2026, including a certificate or scope for nearly every fiscal year and a 2019-03-08 "Partial Suspension of Accreditation". The PDFs themselves sit behind the EDMS CAPTCHA, so read them through `ldeq-edms-download` and then `document-ocr`.

## Script

```bash
S="${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/skills/ldeq-lelap-labs"
L() { uv run -q --no-project python -I "$S/lelap.py" "$@"; }

L health                                          # pages up, config scraped, Total Records == parsed rows
L labs Alliance                                   # directory: AI, LELAP ID (once seen), name, state, mailing city
L scope 242360 --matrix NPW                       # one site's current scope (name, AI, or a LELAP ID already seen)
L scope "ARS Analytical LLC" --method "EPA 6020B"
L who --method "EPA 624.1" --matrix NPW           # labs holding a method today (add --analyte)
L methods 8260                                    # exact method strings on the page; also `analytes <text>`
L check 119652 --method "EPA 6020B" --analyte Lead --matrix NPW --date 2019-04-15
L history 119652 --fy 2019                        # EDMS certificate/amendment/suspension index
L history 119652 --all-docs                       # also PT results, assessments, corrective-action plans
L export lelap_scopes.csv --yes                   # whole statewide table, ~14 MB; refuses without --yes
```

- `--json` goes before or after the subcommand.
- `--refresh` bypasses the one-day cache, which lives under `~/.cache/bayou/ldeq-lelap-labs/`.
- Exit codes:
  - 3: the lab, method or analyte was not found, or the name is ambiguous. Candidates are printed.
  - 5: a source is unavailable.
  - 2: bad arguments, or `export` was run without `--yes`.

## Workflow

1. **Get what the report says.** From the lab report or the data appendix, note:
   - the lab name and address;
   - the matrix: wastewater or effluent is `NPW`, soil, sludge or waste is `SCM`, a stack is `AE`, fish or tissue is `BT`;
   - the method exactly as printed, revision included;
   - the analyte;
   - the sample date (not the analysis or report date).
2. **Resolve the site.** One name can cover several accredited sites with separate AIs and separate scopes. "Alliance Technical Group" has five, located in TX, GA, AL, LA and OR. `check` refuses an ambiguous name and lists the sites. Choose by the address on the report. The directory's city is the *mailing* city, often a corporate office: three of the Alliance sites give Decatur, AL as their mailing address. Match on the physical-location state and the report's address. When the address does not settle it, ask the lab or LDEQ.
3. **Resolve the method and the analyte exactly.** Matching ignores only case and whitespace. **Method revisions are distinct accreditations:**
   - `EPA 624` and `EPA 624.1` are different methods, held by 13 and 31 NPW sites respectively.
   - The `EPA 8260` family alone has 12 strings: 8260, 8260A, 8260B, 8260B (Update III) 1996, 8260C, 8260D, the SIM variants, and others.

   A miss prints the strings that contain your terms. Never choose one unless the report actually used it. If the report's exact method is on no one's scope, that is itself the answer: no lab holds it today.
4. **`check`** prints:
   - **Current status:** `LISTED` (with the Accreditation Type), `METHOD_LISTED_ANALYTE_NOT`, `OTHER_MATRIX_ONLY`, `NOT_LISTED`, or `UNMAPPED` (the scope table has no rows for this site, so its directory name may have drifted from the scope table's; this is never evidence about the analysis). When nothing is listed, it also shows any same-number methods on the lab's scope, labeled as *not equivalent*.
   - **Fiscal year.** LDEQ's fiscal year runs July 1 to June 30 and is named for the year it ends: FY16 is 2015-07-01 to 2016-06-30. Annual renewal falls on July 1 (§4503 "Annual Renewal Date", §5703.A).
   - **EDMS evidence:**
     - that FY's certificate or scope;
     - every later certificate, amendment, suspension or withdrawal up to the sample date;
     - related correspondence: renewal applications, requests to withdraw a method, certified-mail receipts;
     - for context, anything later in the same FY;
     - the document IDs to pull. Pull them with `ldeq-edms-download`, OCR them, and read the scope pages for the matrix, method and analyte in question.
   - **When no certificate is found** for that FY, it says so and drafts the records-request scope.
   - **When the sample date is today or later**, it reports current status only.
5. **Read the documents before you write anything.** The EDMS index shows titles, not scopes.
   - A "Partial Suspension" matters only if it covers this analysis: §5705.A lets LDEQ suspend "in any or all test categories".
   - An "amendment / withdrawal" may have removed exactly the method in question.
   - Annual scopes are reissued every year. The method has to appear on the scope that was in force on the sample date.
6. **Run the applicability screen** (below). Use the word "defect" only after the screen comes back clean.

## What LELAP covers, and what it does not

- **Commercial labs only.** §4501.A.1 applies the program to "commercial laboratories, wherever located, that provide chemical analyses, analytical results, or other test data to the department". §4503 defines a commercial laboratory as one that "performs analyses or tests for third parties for a fee or other compensation".
  - A facility's in-house lab analyzing its own samples is not a commercial lab.
  - Government in-house labs are exempt from the fees but "shall make appropriate application for accreditation" (§4707.B).
  - A permit condition can independently require an accredited lab. Read the permit.
- **The data must go to LDEQ** (§4501.A.1.a–f): data submitted on behalf of a facility, required as part of a permit application, required by an order, required on monitoring reports, required by contract, or otherwise required by regulation.
- **Matrices** (§4501.B): air emissions (`AE`), nonpotable water (`NPW`), solid and chemical material (`SCM`), biological tissue (`BT`). Each matrix is divided into 11 test categories (§4705.B):
  1. metals
  2. air pollutants (including stack sampling and ambient air)
  3. nutrients, minerals and classical wet chemistry
  4. microbiology
  5. bioassay and biomonitoring
  6. organics
  7. dioxins and furans
  8. radiochemistry
  9. asbestos
  10. geotechnical properties of soils
  11. minor conventional parameters: BOD5, oil and grease, TSS, pH, coliform, residual chlorine
- **Field tests are excluded.** "These regulations shall not apply to field tests as defined in LAC 33:I.4503" (§4719.D). §4503 defines a field test as an on-site measurement, including "soil classification, pH, temperature, flow rate, fugitive emissions monitoring of valves, pumps, flanges, etc." A DMR pH or temperature taken in the field, or LDAR monitoring, is therefore not a LELAP question.
- **Other exclusions** (§4501.E):
  1. programs accredited by the Louisiana Department of Health, which covers drinking water. The lab ids on LDH sample results (for example `LA01134`) are LDH certifications; see `bayou:ldh-drinking-water`;
  2. personnel monitoring under LAC 33:XV.430.C, and activities licensed under LAC 33:XV Chapter 3 Subchapter B or NRC rules.
- **LELAP interpretations:**
  - "there is no requirement for the accreditation of the methods producing data in support of Whole Effluent Toxicity (WET) methods";
  - drilling-mud and sediment toxicity "reported to EPA, and not required to be reported to LDEQ, does not require LELAP accreditation";
  - emission testing with a portable analyzer must use ASTM D6522-00(2005);
  - asbestos accreditation for EPA 600/M4-82-020 on an NVLAP scope also grants EPA 600-R-93/116.
- **Status changes:**
  - Accreditation is renewed annually (§5703.A).
  - Fees unpaid for 30 days automatically suspend accreditation, and after 90 days the lab is discredited (§5703.C–D).
  - Suspension or discreditation may cover "any or all test categories", and LDEQ gives notice "by registered or certified letter" (§5705.A–B). This is why certified-mail receipts appear next to suspension notices in EDMS.
  - Interim status "shall not exceed one year" (§4503).

## Accreditation Type is the basis, not a status

The Accreditation Type column holds `LA` (LDEQ is the primary accreditor, about 30,000 rows) or the name of another body: `FL`, `TX`, `OR`, `KS`, `NELAP`, `PA`, `UT`, `NJ`, `MN`, `ISO 17025`, `IL`, `State`, `NH`, `A2LA`, `AIHA`, `AAP`, `ANAB`, `NY`, `NVLAP`. The other bodies' values mark accreditation granted on the strength of that body's accreditation:
- reciprocity for out-of-state labs (§4503 "Reciprocity", §4715);
- interim acceptance (§4713);
- the NELAP chapter (Chapter 59).

A non-`LA` value is still LELAP accreditation. Never describe one as "not accredited by LDEQ".

## Caveats

- **Today's table answers only for today.** It cannot confirm accreditation on a past date. A lab or method missing from it today does not prove the lab lacked accreditation on the sample date: the lab may have dropped the method, moved or closed since then. Both limits print on every `scope`, `who` and `check`.
- **EDMS indexing is uneven:**
  - Most certificates are filed as Permits / Certificate/License/Registration.
  - Some years are filed as Permits with no subtype, such as the FY24 "Laboratory Accreditation Approval Package".
  - Some are filed as Correspondence-Sent ("Scope of Accreditation FY 2015").

  `history` therefore pulls every LELAP-media document and classifies each one by type and description: `certificate`, `status` (suspension, withdrawal, revocation), `related`, or `other` (PT results, assessments, corrective-action plans; shown with `--all-docs`). Check the classification against the titles before relying on the absence of a document.
- **A lab that moved or changed owners** may have its older file under a predecessor AI. If `history` comes up short, search EDMS for the old name with `ldeq-edms-search`.
- **The scope table includes what looks like a test record**: "Test Three" (AI 157565). It is not in the directory. Leave it out of any count of labs.

## Records requests

When EDMS has no certificate for the fiscal year, or the PDFs do not show the analysis, request the following through `la-public-records-request`, using the LDEQ form (https://edms.deq.louisiana.gov/prr/RequestForm.aspx):
- the lab's AI and name;
- "LELAP certificate and scope of accreditation for FYnn";
- any amendment, suspension, withdrawal or discreditation notices dated from July 1 of that FY through the sample date.

`check` prints this scope for you. Program questions go to LDEQ.LabAccreditationProgram@LA.GOV.
