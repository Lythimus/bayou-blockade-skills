# What actually changes a permit

Comment leverage is not proportional to how strongly the comment is felt, how long it is, or
how many people sign it. It tracks one thing: **how expensive the comment makes it for the
agency to issue the permit unchanged.**

Agencies must consider and respond to *significant* comments; failing to do so is grounds for
a decision to be set aside as arbitrary and capricious. Significance comes from specificity —
enough detail to put the agency on notice of the issue and give it a chance to address the
substance. Heat does not create a response obligation. A precise defect does.

Sort every finding into a tier before drafting. Tier 1 leads the letter, Tier 4 is cut.

---

## Tier 1 — Changes the permit

These create legal exposure for the agency itself. Fixing them is cheaper than defending them,
which is exactly why they work.

| Finding type | Why it lands |
|---|---|
| **A mandatory application element is missing** | The application should not have been deemed complete. Cite the regulation that requires it and note the completeness determination date. Forces either a waiver on the record or a remand. |
| **A permit condition cannot be complied with as written** | An undefined baseline, an undefined term, a cross-reference to something that does not exist. An unenforceable condition is an appeal issue the agency created for itself. |
| **The permit cites a regulatory subsection whose predicate is not satisfied** | E.g. a relocation provision that presupposes an already-approved location, where no approved location exists. Either the citation is wrong or the permit is. Both are the agency's problem. |
| **A limit the agency relied on exists only in narrative** | If the tonnage basis, throughput, or control assumption appears in a briefing sheet or spreadsheet but not in enforceable conditions, the demonstration rests on nothing. |
| **Internal contradiction that changes an applicability determination** | Two throughput figures, two hours bases, a speciation that exceeds its own cap. Especially potent where a number sits just under a major-source, MACT, or PSD trigger. |
| **Monitoring cannot verify the limit it is attached to** | Opacity does not measure benzene. Annual averages cannot constrain hourly exposure. Name the limit, name the monitoring, show the gap. |
| **The agency's own staff raised the problem in writing and it was not resolved** | An unresolved internal technical objection in the record is the strongest single item a comment can carry. Quote it exactly. |

**Drafting rule:** each Tier 1 finding ends in the specific condition that cures it. The
agency's cheapest path must be to write the condition you drafted.

## Tier 2 — Builds the record

These may not change this permit. They preserve the issue, constrain the next one, and create
the appeal.

- **Issue preservation.** An issue not raised in comments generally cannot be raised on appeal.
  Every colorable objection goes in the letter, even briefly, even ones unlikely to win.
- **The agency's own prior regulation, decision, or practice, used against this action.** A
  regulation the agency wrote for this exact activity and is now bypassing. A prior denial on
  facts that have since gotten worse. A per-event mechanism the applicant used for years and
  then abandoned. Very hard to answer with boilerplate.
- **Documented compliance and enforcement history against a blank or minimal disclosure.**
  Consent decrees, penalties, incident reports, prior withdrawals of this same permit number.
  The gap between the record and the disclosure is the finding.
- **Requests to place specific documents in the record.** Native modeling files, a legible copy
  of an illegible page, a referenced worksheet that is not in the docket. Cheap to ask,
  awkward to refuse, and the refusal is itself appealable.
- **Public-notice defects.** Misclassification, inconsistent permit numbers, absence from the
  agency's own tracker, notices returned undeliverable. These go to whether the comment period
  was meaningful at all.

## Tier 3 — Moves discretion

These do not compel anything. They influence choices the agency has room to make.

- **Standing and affected-person narrative.** Required for appeal rights; also the difference
  between a document from a stakeholder and a document from nobody in particular.
- **Hearing request with an actual showing.** Hearings are typically discretionary on a finding
  of "significant public interest" against no defined threshold. Argue the showing — population
  covered, parishes affected, permit duration — rather than just requesting.
- **Health mechanism tied to the permit's named pollutants.** What the pollutant does inside a
  body, then who is standing in front of it. Mechanism is technical and must be answered;
  status alone is sympathy and gets a sympathetic non-answer.
- **Cumulative burden and environmental-justice data.** Strongest when the record's own gap
  makes the analysis impossible — the inability to run the screen becomes the finding.
- **Alternatives that were never evaluated.** Name specific, commercially available
  alternatives. A search returning zero mentions of any of them is evidence.

## Tier 4 — Costs more than it earns

Cut these even when true.

- **Anything outside the agency's authority for this permit.** Property values, traffic, and
  land use in an air permit comment. Raise them with the body that has jurisdiction instead.
- **General opposition with no record citation.** Counts as volume, not substance.
- **Emotional characterization that invites the NIMBY filing.** Assassination, poisoning,
  colonization. The underlying point survives without the phrasing; the phrasing is what lets
  a permit writer classify the whole document as emotional and answer with boilerplate.
- **Any factual claim you cannot source**, including a plausible number from a snippet that
  never resolved to a primary document. One unsourceable claim discredits the sourced ones
  around it.
- **Mischaracterized case law.** Worse than no citation. Opposing counsel reads these filings.
- **Attribution you have not disambiguated.** A penalty that might belong to a differently
  named corporate affiliate stays out until the attribution is resolved.

## Structural multipliers

Independent of content, these raise the response burden:

1. **Number the comments.** Agencies produce itemized response summaries; numbered comments
   force itemized answers and make a skipped one visible.
2. **Ask for itemized response explicitly**, in the relief section.
3. **Separate record facts from outside facts** visibly. It signals the whole document was
   checked and denies the easy "unsupported assertions" dismissal.
4. **Include a candor section.** Unresolved items stated as open questions preserve the issues
   without asserting unverified facts, and demonstrate everything else was verified.
5. **Request written notice of the final decision.** Starts the appeal clock cleanly.
6. **File before the deadline.** A late comment is not in the administrative record, and
   nothing else on this page matters if that happens.
