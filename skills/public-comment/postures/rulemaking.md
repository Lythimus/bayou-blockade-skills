# Posture: rulemaking

An agency proposing a rule of general applicability: a federal NPRM under the APA, a Louisiana
Register notice of intent, or a waiver or exemption adopted by regulation. The action governs
every future case, not one facility.

Worked example in `~/Documents/personal/petro/`: `starbase-la/faa-waiver/PUBLIC-COMMENT.md`
(FAA-2026-8614, a general waiver of 13 environmental statutes for commercial space licensing),
with its research memo `context-and-precedent.md`.

---

## Audience

Two readers:
1. **The rule-writing staff**, who must address significant comments in the final rule's
   preamble.
2. **A reviewing court**, asking whether the agency considered the relevant factors and
   stayed inside its statutory authority.

What moves them:
- the enabling statute's own conditions;
- whether the preamble matches the regulatory text;
- whether the agency's own record supports the finding the rule depends on;
- whether the cost-benefit and small-entity analyses exist.

The agency's own solicitations matter too. **An NPRM that asks numbered questions is telling you
exactly which comments it will have to engage.** Answer them by letter and number ("Answering
FAA's question (c)…"), and put the evidence the agency asked for under that heading.

## Interest (not a gate)

Anyone may comment on a rulemaking. 5 U.S.C. 553(c) gives "interested persons an opportunity to
participate," with no aggrieved-person test. **Do not stop** because the commenter has no
connection to a particular site.

**Do not cite La. R.S. 30:2050.21 or any permit-appeal statute.**

Section heading: **"Commenter and interest."**

Interest still matters, for two reasons: a later challenge to the final rule needs Article III
standing, and a comment from someone with a concrete stake reads differently. The pattern that
worked:
1. **Concede distance first.** ("I don't live in Vermilion Parish… If this were a site-specific
   license, I'd have a much thinner claim.")
2. **Tie the interest to what the rule actually does.** A general rule reaches the commenter
   through the provisions it changes. For example: the waived CZMA consistency review covers the
   commenter's own coastal parish; a waived general-conformity check covers the parishes where
   their family lives.
3. **Mention prior use of the process, briefly, if the profile supports it.**

Household health facts appear only as the reason the commenter tracks a provision. They are
never a claim that the rule's effects reach the household, unless that can be shown.

## What each numbered comment ends in

**An edit to the rule or its record.** Any of these endpoints works:
- **Rule-text edit.** "Delete 'including, but not limited to' from the description of the
  class"; "revise § 400.3(c) to state which reading is intended."
- **Scoping.** "Limit § 400.3(a) to federal launch and reentry sites and retain §§ 420.15(b),
  433.7 … for non-federal sites."
- **Make and publish the missing statutory finding**, with its factual basis.
- **Docket a missing record item** (consultation responses, the RIA), plus a **supplemental
  comment period** on it.
- **Withdraw** or **do not finalize as proposed.**

"This rule is bad policy" is not an endpoint.

## Leverage tiers

The posture-neutral principle and the Tier 4 cut list are in `references/leverage.md`.

### Tier 1: changes the final rule

| Finding type | Why it lands |
|---|---|
| **The enabling statute's precondition is not met on the agency's own record** | Example: the statute requires the Secretary to *decide* a requirement "is not necessary," but the preamble says it "may not be necessary in some or all circumstances." This is the rule's own legal foundation, and it can be fixed only by making the finding or narrowing the rule. |
| **The operative text has two irreconcilable readings** | An ambiguous self-executing provision cannot be applied, and it invites a challenge on day one. |
| **The preamble and the regulatory text do not match** | The preamble lists 13 laws and the text waives a different set. Whichever controls, the other misinforms the public. |
| **A required analysis is missing or empty** | A significant regulatory action with no quantified costs or benefits, or an RFA certification with no factual basis. |
| **The agency's own concession undercuts the rule's scope** | For example: "At federal sites this merely shifts responsibility." That concession is the argument for scoping. |

### Tier 2: builds the record

- **Evidence the agency explicitly asked for**, under its own question number.
- **The agency's own prior practice**, such as mitigation it obtained through the mechanisms
  being waived, or the record of an analogous waiver authority.
- **Missing consultation records** (interagency, tribal) and a request to docket them.
- **Unintended legal consequences.** Example: waiving a *permission* mechanism while the
  underlying *prohibition* stays in force, which increases the regulated party's exposure.

### Tier 3: moves discretion

- Interest narrative.
- Site-specific worked examples that show how differently situated sites fare under a general rule.
- A request for a supplemental comment period or public meeting, with record reasons.

## Health-mechanism section

**Only where the rule itself governs exposure**, for example an emission standard or a
monitoring rule. For a procedural or waiver rule, fold health into the interest statement as the
reason the commenter tracks a provision. Do not write a standalone section.

## Identifiers (RE block and `\filingid`)

- Docket number · RIN · Notice number · agency.
- The rule title in italics with its Fed. Reg. cite (volume, first page, date).
- The comment deadline.
- For Louisiana: the La. Register volume and issue, the LAC cite being amended, and the
  agency's notice of intent.

**Citation convention:** cite the proposal by printed Fed. Reg. page ("91 Fed. Reg. 47998").

## Relief-authority line

"Under 5 U.S.C. 553(c), and in view of the conditions Congress placed on [the enabling
authority]…"

List the relief in this order:
1. Do not finalize as drafted.
2. In the alternative, scope the rule.
3. The specific text and record fixes.
4. A supplemental comment period, if record items are missing.
5. Itemized response to each numbered comment.
6. Written notice of the final rule.

For a Louisiana rule, cite the Louisiana APA notice-and-comment section only after verifying it
with `bayou:la-rs-search`.

## Response owed and terminal clock

- **Response:** the agency must address significant comments. Under 553(c), the final rule
  incorporates "a concise general statement of [its] basis and purpose," and failing to respond
  to a significant comment is a standard arbitrary-and-capricious ground. Number the comments,
  and ask for responses identified by number.
- **Clock:** the challenge window for a final rule depends on the governing review statute.
  Some statutes set a short petition-for-review period, and otherwise the general federal
  limitations period applies. **Look up the specific review provision for the enabling statute
  before stating any deadline.** Never assume 30 or 60 days. Separately, 5 U.S.C. 553(e) gives
  any interested person the right to petition later for amendment or repeal.

## Posture-specific QA

- Every question the NPRM solicits that the letter's evidence bears on is answered under its
  own label.
- Every numbered comment ends in a rule-text, scope, record, or procedural edit.
- No permit-appeal statute, "permit writer," or "Response Summary" language appears.
- Docket number, RIN, and Fed. Reg. cite appear in the RE block, and all Fed. Reg. pin cites
  are to printed pages.
