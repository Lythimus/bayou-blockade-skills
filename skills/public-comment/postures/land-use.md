# Posture: local land use

A parish or municipal body deciding a land-use matter in any Louisiana parish:
- a **rezoning** or a change to the zoning text or map;
- a **subdivision plat** before a planning commission;
- a **variance** or appeal before a board of adjustment;
- a **land-use ordinance** or a local land-use permit.

This file is drawn from statutes and opinions read directly, and
it marks what isn't established. **It is not legal advice.** Have a Louisiana lawyer check any
deadline before relying on it. Statutes were read on legis.la.gov on 2026-11-03. Each case's status
is in `references/louisiana-hooks.md`.

---

## First: which law governs here

Answer this before quoting any notice count. State statutes set the default, and they don't reach
every parish in the same way.

| Where the land is | Start with | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| Unincorporated parish | R.S. 33:4780.40–.50 | **Rapides, Jefferson, Sabine and Webster are excluded** (§ 4780.40). Their zoning authority wasn't checked. |
| Parish with a home rule charter | The charter, then the local code | § 4780.50(C): the Subpart doesn't change how a home rule parish structures and distributes its powers. Whether its notice rules still bind such a parish wasn't established. Quote them only after checking the charter and code, and cite the local rule where it exists. |
| Inside a municipality | R.S. 33:4721–4729 | The notice, mail and voting rules differ from the parish rules. § 4780.50(B) keeps the two apart unless the municipality adopts the parish rules. |
| Any plat | R.S. 33:101–119, the local subdivision regulations | Applies where a planning commission exists and has adopted regulations (§ 33:112). |

La. Const. art. VI, § 17 is the source of local power, "[s]ubject to uniform procedures established
by law". Get the local zoning code, the subdivision regulations, the body's rules, and the notice
itself. A local code can add to the state floor, and a code requirement it didn't follow can be fatal
(*Tassin*). Some parishes have no zoning at all, so check before arguing about a district.

Find the case's path and dates with `bayou:parish-council-agendas track` where that skill covers the
parish. Elsewhere, use the parish's own agendas and official journal. Parcels and districts come from
`bayou:parish-assessor-gis`, also limited to the parishes it lists.

## Audience

Who reads this letter depends on the instrument:
1. **Commission or board members and staff**, who are mostly unpaid local appointees reading a staff
   report. They respond to the code's own criteria.
2. **The governing body**, elected, deciding in public. It answers to the neighbors in the room as
   well as to the code.
3. **The body's attorney**, who reads for a procedural defect that could void the action.
4. **A reviewing judge, who starts from deference.** *Alexander v. St. James Parish*, 2025-C-00866
   (La. 3/6/26), is the controlling recent statement, and it went against the challengers:
   - "Courts will not and cannot substitute their judgment for that of the legislative authority"
     (quoting *Palermo*).
   - When "there is room for two opinions, action is not arbitrary or capricious when exercised
     honestly and upon due consideration" (quoting *King*).
   - The parish's interpretation of its own land-use plan received deference. A claim that it "failed
     to follow its own ordinance" lost when it was really a disagreement over what the ordinance
     meant. Footnote 13 adds that an ambiguous land-use ordinance is read to allow the least
     restricted use.

**So how do these fights actually turn?** Mostly on the vote, or on a procedural step that was or
wasn't taken. Few cases are won on the theory that a use is a bad idea. Write for the vote, and put
in the record what a judge would need to see if the vote goes wrong.

## Interest (not a gate at the hearing)

Section heading: **"Who I am and my interest."**

R.S. 33:4780.43 and 33:4724(B) give "parties in interest" an opportunity to be heard, and R.S.
42:14(D) requires a public comment period before a vote on an agenda item. Don't stop for lack of
proximity. Say plainly how far you live from the site, what you can observe, and what the decision
would change for you.

Standing matters later, in court. There the words differ by instrument:
- **Rezoning or ordinance:** a plaintiff needs a real interest (C.C.P. art. 681 was the basis the
  trial court used in *Coogan*). Competition alone isn't enough (*Lauer v. City of Kenner*, followed in
  *1900 Highway 190* on an inverse condemnation claim).
- **Variance:** a "person aggrieved" under R.S. 33:4780.47(A) or 33:4727(E)(1). Neighbors qualified
  under the municipal statute in *Cupit*.
- **What this means for the letter:** state distance, ownership and the concrete effect as facts.
  Don't argue standing, and never invent proximity.

**Never cite La. R.S. 30:2050.21, "aggrieved person" as an LDEQ term, or R.S. 49:964.** The first is
LDEQ's permit appeal. The second is state-agency review, and the appeal court in *Alexander* withdrew
a reference to it on rehearing.

## What each numbered request ends in

Each request ends in something this body can do at this meeting, or can order done:
- **A corrected step:** re-notice and re-hear, or hold action until a required report exists.
- **A record item:** a document placed in the record before the vote, or an answer from staff or the
  applicant.
- **A stated finding** on a criterion the code names, such as plan consistency or hardship.
- **A plat outcome:** disapproval with the ground stated on the record (R.S. 33:113), or an
  improvement required as a condition precedent, with any bond (R.S. 33:112(D)).
- **Deferral** to a stated date, tied to a stated missing item.
- **Denial**, or an amendment to ordinance text the drafter can use.
- **Transmittal** of the letter to the body that acts next, and **written notice** of the decision.

A condition on a rezoning itself isn't a safe request: whether the body may attach one wasn't
established and depends on the local code. Look for a conditional-use or planned-district route first.

Worked examples, invented for illustration:
- *Notice:* "The first publication ran 8 days before the hearing. R.S. 33:4780.43 requires at least
  15. Do not hold the hearing or vote on this notice. Re-publish and reset the hearing."
- *Sequence:* "The agenda shows no final report from the zoning commission on this matter. R.S.
  33:4780.45 bars the council from holding its hearing or acting until it has one. Place the report
  in the record, or defer until it exists."
- *Plat:* "Section 4.2 of the subdivision regulations requires a second access for this lot count, and
  the plat shows one. Disapprove and state that ground on the record under R.S. 33:113, or require
  the access as a condition precedent under R.S. 33:112(D)."
- *Variance:* "The application states higher construction cost and no other difficulty. Find on the
  record what practical difficulty or unnecessary hardship R.S. 33:4780.46(B)(3)(c) rests on, or deny."

"This rezoning is out of character" is not a request. "Find on the record how this change accords with
the comprehensive plan, as R.S. 33:4780.42 and .43 require, naming the plan element relied on" is.

## Leverage tiers

The posture-neutral principle and the Tier 4 cut list are in `references/leverage.md`.

### Tier 1: could void the action or stop the vote

These don't depend on what the body thinks of the project. Show the arithmetic. A count of days is not
an interpretation of the code. That distinction is this file's reading of *Alexander* and *Tassin*,
and no case read here states it.

| Finding type | Why it lands |
|---|---|
| **Published notice falls short** | Parish council: once a week in three different weeks, at least 15 days from first publication to hearing (§ 4780.43). Zoning commission: at least three publications, at least 10 days (§ 4780.45). Municipal: at least three publications, at least 10 days (§§ 4724(B), 4726(A)). Plat: at least one publication, at least 5 days (§ 33:113). Check the local rule too. |
| **Owner notice is missing** | Parish rezoning: good-faith certified mail to owners of record at least 10 days before the hearing, unless more than ten parcels are involved (§ 4780.43). Municipal: regular mail, with limits (§ 4724(C)–(D)). |
| **A notice step in the local code wasn't followed** | *Tassin*: "Failure to give the required notice is fatal to the rezoning ordinance despite the fact that some property owners had actual notice", and past non-compliance waives nothing. Read: posted signs required by the local ordinance. |
| **The body acted out of order** | The council may not hold its hearing or act until it has the zoning commission's final report (§§ 4780.45, 4726(A)). |
| **Open Meetings defect** | Agenda posted under 24 hours before the meeting, not counting Saturdays, Sundays and legal holidays, an item not described with reasonable specificity, an item added without a unanimous vote, or no comment before the vote (R.S. 42:19(A)(1)(b), 42:14(D)). Planning and zoning commissions are public bodies (42:13(A)(3)). Voidable; suit within 60 days (42:24). A parish board of adjustment's meetings "may be open" when it acts quasi-judicially (§ 4780.46(B)(1)), so whether this row reaches a variance hearing isn't established. |
| **A protest changes the vote needed** | A protest signed and acknowledged by owners of 20% of the area in the change, or within 200 feet of the district's boundaries, requires a favorable vote of a majority of the parish body's members (§ 4780.44), or of municipal members present and voting (§ 4725). **It is not a two-thirds rule.** It helps where absences could otherwise pass the change. |
| **Mandatory referral was skipped** | Once a master plan is adopted and filed, a street, park, public building or public utility, publicly or privately owned, needs commission approval of location, character and extent. Override takes a recorded two-thirds vote of the entire membership (R.S. 33:109(A)). A note on the section points to Acts 2004, No. 859, § 2 on applicability, which wasn't read, so check it first. |
| **A plat lacks what the regulations require, or had no hearing** | Regulations set the elements (§ 33:112). No plat may be acted on without a hearing (§ 33:113), except a waiver for small subdivisions in parishes or municipalities over 150,000 people, if the regulations provide it. |

### Tier 2: builds the record

- **Comprehensive plan consistency.** Zoning regulations "shall be made in accordance with a
  comprehensive plan" (§§ 4780.42, 4780.43). Where a commission has adopted a master plan, the
  governing authority "shall consider" it before adopting inconsistent local laws (R.S. 33:109(B)).
  That is a duty to consider, not to obey. Note that Acts 2004, No. 859, § 2 addresses this section's
  applicability and wasn't read. Check it.
- **The statutory factors.** § 4780.42 names congestion, fire safety, health and general welfare,
  transportation, water, sewerage, schools, parks, a district's character and suitability, and
  conserving building values. Tie each fact to one.
- **Facts the body can't later say it didn't have.** The test asks whether action was "in disregard of
  the facts and circumstances", and whether "substantial evidence" could justify it (*King*;
  *Alexander*, quoting the trial court's statement of the test). A specific fact in the record is the
  only thing that can make that argument available.
- **Every document you rely on, attached.** See "What filing preserves".
- **The applicant's own filings against its presentation.** Quote the application, staff report or plat.
- **Requests for missing material:** the staff report, the application, the plat, and studies the code
  requires.

### Tier 3: moves discretion

- **Turnout and named local effects.** *Alexander* recites that the commission heard "one citizen
  comment in favor of the project and none against". Silence gets written down too.
- **A deferral tied to a specific missing item.**
- **Questions at the hearing** through `bayou:hearing-prep`.
- **Plan and policy arguments** for council members who may share them, kept separate from the Tier 1
  defects.

### Tier 4 for this posture (cut, in addition to the shared list)

- **Requests that the parish regulate emissions or second-guess a state permit.** R.S. 33:4780.49 says
  no local governing authority shall "restrict, conflict with, interfere with, or supersede" the state's
  power over environmental laws, or activities operating under state or federal permits. Argue land-use
  compatibility under the code's own factors instead. A local code may itself list emissions as a
  factor, as St. James's did in *Alexander*. Cite the code if so. Whether this limit reaches a
  home-rule parish or a municipality wasn't established.
- **The *Save Ourselves* IT factors as if they bound the parish.** They were written for the state
  agency. Whether they reach a local land-use decision wasn't established.
- **"The parish misread its own ordinance" as the lead argument.** See *Alexander*. Where it must be
  made, show why no reasonable reading supports the parish.
- **The *Alexander* court of appeal opinion** (24-557, La. App. 5 Cir.). It was vacated. So were its
  "de novo review" and failure-to-follow reasoning.
- **Competition or a business rival's harm** (*Lauer*; *1900 Highway 190*).
- **"Spot zoning" as a label.** Give the facts. The label wasn't researched for this file.

Property values and traffic, which `leverage.md` cuts from an air permit comment, **are in scope here**
where the local code or § 4780.42 reaches them.

## Health-mechanism section

**No standalone section, unless the local code makes health or emissions a criterion.** Health enters as
§ 4780.42's "promote health and the general welfare", tied to location: distance to homes and schools,
and what the new district would allow there. If the code lists a factor, use one short section keyed to
that factor, and no emission limit.

## Identifiers (RE block and `\filingid`)

The body's full name · planning case number · ordinance or file number if assigned · agenda item ·
hearing date · applicant and property description exactly as noticed · current and requested district ·
plat name or variance docket. Name private owners only as the notice or record itself names them.

Keep `\filingid` to the case and ordinance numbers plus the hearing date. Numbers often change between
commission and council, so give both once known.

## Relief-authority line

"Under La. Const. art. VI, § 17 and [the governing statute and code section], I request that the
[body]:"

List the relief in this order:
1. Correct the procedural step, or defer until it is corrected.
2. Deny, or disapprove with the ground stated.
3. In the alternative, the disclosures, findings or plat conditions, each tied to a numbered request.
4. Enter this letter and its exhibits in the record of this case.
5. Transmit them to the body that acts next.
6. Give written notice of the decision, and for a board of adjustment, of the date it is filed in the
   board's office.

## What filing preserves

Whether a letter preserves anything for a court depends on the instrument. **Assume nothing is preserved
unless it reached the body that decides, before it decides.**

| Instrument | What the statute says | Consequence for the letter |
|---|---|---|
| **Plat** | R.S. 33:101.1, amended by Acts 2025, No. 277: approval is legislative, and review "shall be confined to the record established before" the body "whose decision is being appealed". Grounds: arbitrary and capricious, unreasonable, excessive, or due process. | **The record is what counts.** Anything left out is likely gone, so attach it. This is the strongest preservation rule in this posture. |
| **Variance or board appeal** | R.S. 33:4780.47 and 33:4727(E): a verified petition, then certiorari on the board's return. The court *may* take additional evidence if necessary. For parishes, "any opponent before the board" is a party to the court proceeding (§ 4780.47(E)). Testimony is taken down (§ 4780.46(B)(1), § 4727(C)(1)). | Appear, by letter and in person, and identify yourself as an opponent. Whether a letter alone makes someone an "opponent before the board" wasn't established. |
| **Rezoning or zoning ordinance** | R.S. 33:4780.40 and 33:4721 list the grounds for review and say the right "shall not be limited". Neither confines review to a record. Before 2026, cases called this "a de novo proceeding", and *Shaw* (2016) said it is "not an appeal" scrutinizing "the record below". *Alexander* says "[t]here should be no de novo review", while noting those cases used the phrase differently. | **Whether a challenger may add evidence in court after *Alexander* isn't established.** No rule read says an issue not raised at the hearing is waived, and none says it isn't. Raise everything and attach everything anyway. |
| **Open Meetings claim** | R.S. 42:24–.26 | Nothing need be raised at the meeting under the text read. Raising it may let the body cure it. |

Send the letter to **each** body that will act. Whether a letter to the commission reaches the council's
record wasn't established.

## Response owed and terminal clock

**No response duty was found.** No statute read requires a local body to answer comments. A plat
disapproval must state its ground on the record (§ 33:113). A commission must report findings and
recommendations to the council (§ 4780.45), and a board of adjustment must keep minutes showing each
member's vote (§ 4780.46(B)(1)). Ask for an itemized response anyway, and ask that the letter be sent on.

| Event | Clock | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Board of adjustment decision | Petition within **30 days after the decision is filed in the board's office** (§§ 4780.47(A), 4727(E)(1)). Filing doesn't stay the decision; the court may restrain (§ 4780.47(B)). | Text read. Ask for the filing date in writing. |
| Administrative officer's zoning decision | Appeal to the board within **30 days**, as its rules provide, with a stay subject to the officer's certificate (§§ 4780.46(B)(2), 4727). | Text read. Check the board's rules. |
| Open Meetings violation | Suit within **60 days** of the action (R.S. 42:24). Any person may sue (42:25(C)), and a prevailing party recovers fees (42:26(C)). | Text read. |
| Plat | The commission must act within **60 days of submission**, or the plat is deemed approved unless the applicant waives (§ 33:113). That clock runs against the commission. | Text read. Deadline to challenge: **not established**. § 33:101.1 sets none, so check the regulations. |
| Rezoning or ordinance | **Not established.** No period was found in the statutes read. *Coogan* (1980) held the 30 days in § 4727 didn't apply to a suit over a parish council's zoning decision where there was no board of adjustment. "A reasonable time" and "[s]ix months is beyond" come only from a dissent there. R.S. 33:175's 30 days is for annexation ordinances. | **Plan as if 30 days from the vote may be the limit, and see a lawyer at once.** That is caution, not the law. Check the local code. |
| A local appeal step (commission to council) | Set by local code. In *Alexander* the challengers appealed to the council, then sued. | Whether skipping it bars a suit wasn't established. Don't skip it. |

## Not established

Keep these out of the letter as assertions, and raise them with counsel:
- the deadline to challenge a rezoning or a plat;
- whether evidence can be added in court in a rezoning challenge after *Alexander*;
- whether an issue not raised at a hearing is waived;
- whether a written comment alone makes someone an "opponent before the board";
- whether a comment to the commission is in the council's record;
- whether a rezoning may carry conditions, absent a code provision;
- how the zoning subparts apply to each home rule parish and each excluded parish;
- how Acts 2004, No. 859, § 2 limits R.S. 33:109;
- how local rules on non-zoning ordinances work, including introduction, publication and effective date, which
  depend on each charter.

## Posture-specific QA

- The governing law was identified for this parish, including home rule and the four excluded parishes.
  The local code was read, and every notice count cites the rule that sets it.
- Each date was counted, the count is shown, and the published dates come from the official journal.
- Every request can be done by this body at this stage. Nothing asks a commission to do what only the
  council can.
- No 2050.21, "permit condition", "Response Summary" or *Save Ourselves* language, and no vacated *Alexander*
  court of appeal cite.
- No emission limit or second-guessing of a state permit, unless the local code makes it a criterion.
- Protest language says majority, not two-thirds.
- Exhibits are attached, not linked. The letter asks to enter them in the record, and goes to each
  acting body with proof of delivery.
- Plat letters are complete, because review is confined to the record.
- The report back to the user states the clock, or says plainly that it isn't established and that a
  lawyer should check it.
