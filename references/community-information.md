# Community information in drafts

Apply this reference when a draft uses privately supplied community information, personal
experiences, sensitive locations, or claims to represent a community's position.

- Preserve attribution, anonymity, and sharing preferences already supplied, including the
  intended audience or channel. Permission to use an account in one context does not imply
  permission to identify its author or reuse it everywhere.
- Distinguish Joseph's analysis, a resident's account, and an organization's stated position.
  Attribute observations as accounts; do not turn one person's experience into a claim of
  community consensus or imply authority to speak for an organization without support.
- If sharing permission or representation is unresolved, place a short editorial flag beside
  the affected passage, such as `[Sharing scope unresolved: confirm use of this account in
  the public post.]` Continue the rest of the draft. Keep sensitive details out of the flag;
  use a placeholder for material whose disclosure is unresolved. For memes, put the flag in
  the accompanying draft notes and use a safe placeholder or omit that material from renders.
- Resolve localized flags through established review arrangements. Reuse preferences and
  approvals already given; do not repeatedly request confirmation or add a separate approval
  gate. Unresolved private details should remain placeholders in externally stored drafts.
- Ordinary use of public agency records, public statements, and demographic statistics
  requires no new community approval step. Public statements still need accurate attribution;
  they do not establish broader community agreement. Existing protections for burial
  locations remain applicable, including when a source is public.

This guidance adds no governance questionnaire, consent registry, outreach process, incident
intake, or community-history workflow. Existing drafting, review, and sending rules still apply.
