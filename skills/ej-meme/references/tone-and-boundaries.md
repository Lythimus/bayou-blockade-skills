# Tone and boundaries

## What a meme is for

**Make them laugh, then make them think about what's being given up.** A meme is one idea:
one fact, one trade, one feeling. It does not tell the whole story. That's the post's job,
or the comment letter's. A meme that tries to explain the permit dies in the feed.

Every meme should leave the reader with a one-line **bum deal**: what the community gives
up, and what the company gets for it.

> A tax break for them → an ammonia plant next to you.
> A $90K fine for them → the safety violations stay for you.
> "Minimal impacts" for them → more water in your street for you.

If you can't write that line for a draft, the draft doesn't have a point yet.

## Punch up

- **Targets:** companies, agencies, decisions, public statements, permit filings.
- **Never targets:** residents, workers at the plant, neighbors who support the project,
  people who took the jobs or the buyouts. The meme is on their side even when they
  disagree.
- **Public officials:** only for their public acts (votes, statements, rulings), and only
  with the act sourced. No jokes about their looks, families or private lives.
- The person in the template picture stands in for the company. Residents appear as
  themselves, as the reasonable ones.

## Truth rules

Satire gets a pass on exaggerated *tone*, not on invented *facts*. A meme that gets one
fact wrong hands the company a reason to dismiss everything else.

1. **Every number traces to a source.** The CSV's `Source` column names the doc and page or
   section. No source, no number.
2. **No invented events.** The old CSVs had "the hospital is expanding its respiratory
   ward" with no source anywhere. If it didn't happen, it doesn't go in a meme, even as a
   joke, because a reader can't tell which part is the joke.
3. **No fabricated quotes from real entities.** Paraphrase in the meme's voice: "Acme Tanks when
   residents mention…" is fine. `Acme Tanks: "We don't care about benzene"` is not. Real quotes
   go in quotation marks only when sourced, and quoted exactly ("very minimal impacts").
4. **Keep the hedges the source has.** "Permitted to release up to" ≠ "releases". "Could"
   ≠ "will". "Estimated" stays.
5. **Same fact, same number, every time.** The old CSVs cited one pipeline rupture as
   sending 31 people to the hospital in one meme and 37 in another. Pick the sourced figure
   and use it everywhere.
6. **Recompute conversions** (see plain-language.md). "4,900% above" is 50 times, not 49
   and not 5.

## Off-limits

- Private individuals by name or recognizable photo, including people quoted at hearings
  and named in comment records.
- Children's faces, or health conditions tied to identifiable people.
- Death, cancer or illness jokes where the victim is the punchline. The cause can be the
  punchline; the people harmed never are.
- Racial caricature, or any template that leans on a stereotype. Environmental-justice
  history (segregation, locked gates, Cancer Alley) is told straight, with the company or
  decision as the target.
- Anything that reads as a threat or wishes harm, even as a joke. Nextdoor bans joking
  violence outright (see below). That includes "burn it down", fire jokes aimed at people,
  and "someone should…".
- Doctored company logos, fake press releases, fake agency notices, or anything that could
  be mistaken for official company or agency material. That's an impersonation risk, and
  it gives the company a legitimate complaint.
- National partisan framing (party names, national politicians). Keep it local.

## Photos

A photo in a template slot is a factual claim, the same as a number. A reader takes it to
be what the caption says it is.

- **It shows what the caption says it shows:** the actual facility, site or place, from
  the time the caption implies. No stand-in refinery, no stock smokestack, no photo of a
  different plant "for illustration". A meme exposed as using the wrong plant discredits
  every true thing next to it, and Nextdoor counts it as manipulated media.
- **Don't alter it** beyond cropping and resizing. No added smoke, no recoloring, no
  pasted-in objects.
- **Nobody recognizable.** The off-limits list above covers photos as well as names:
  residents, workers, children, hearing speakers. Officials only as part of a sourced
  public act, and even then a photo of the facility usually makes the point better.
- **No company or agency logos**, cropped or whole. That's the doctored-logo and
  impersonation problem again.
- **Write down where it came from** (`Overlay_Source`): who took it, when, and the license
  or public-domain basis. If you can't say, don't use it.

## Nextdoor rules that bear on memes

Nextdoor's Community Guidelines have five headings: **be respectful to your neighbors, do
not discriminate, discuss important topics in the right place, use your true identity, do
not engage in harmful activity.** What matters for memes:

- **Violence, even as a joke, is prohibited.** Its examples include "suggesting, showing,
  threatening, or glorifying violence."
- **Public shaming counts as disrespectful behavior.** Aim at the organization's conduct,
  not at shaming a named person.
- **The misinformation policy covers political misinformation, including manipulated
  media.** Another reason not to doctor logos or fake documents.
- **Keep it local.** Non-local national partisan topics belong in Groups, not the main
  feed. A local facility and local agency decisions are squarely in scope.
- **Enforcement:** reported posts go to neighborhood volunteer Leads and Nextdoor staff.
  Content can be removed and accounts suspended. A single post that crosses a line can
  cost the account that runs the whole campaign.

Sources: Nextdoor's Q4 2025 terms-of-service report to the New York Attorney General
([PDF](https://ag.ny.gov/sites/default/files/social-media-policy-report/2025-q4-nextdoor-inc-policy.pdf)),
and the current guidelines at https://help.nextdoor.com/s/article/community-guidelines.
Re-check that page before a big campaign; the rules change.

**What they share:**
- A reaction template carries the emotion, so the caption only has to state the fact.
- **One** concrete number or lived detail: "chemical smell in your bedroom", "4th fire in
  2 years", "$90,000 fine", "20%".
- The company is named, and the claim needs no background to understand.
- 7–22 words, mostly 12–17. The longest ones (Rick Dalton box 1, Boardroom) are the ones
  qa.py flags as small on a phone.

**Standalone versions.** The same jokes, readable with no post:
- "The Pelican Bayou tank farm when neighbors mention they smell benzene in their homes"
- "The ammonia plant planned for Pelican Bayou can't have evacuation problems / if it never
  mentions the neighborhood next door"
- "The state environmental agency's job: protect communities / The state environmental
  agency: calls residents' concerns 'unacceptable'"

## Before / after: rewrites of old failures

Same fictional names and numbers as above; the failure patterns are real.

**1. The wall of text (This Is Fine).** Original top box:
"Ratepayers paying $1.5B for a tech company's data center power while the utility pockets
$80M/year for 30 years". 16 words; on a phone it rendered 7px tall.
Rewrite (Trade Offer), with the same figures:
- Box 1: "$80 million a year for 30 years"
- Box 2: "the $1.5 billion bill"
  (the template already prints "i receive:" / "you receive:")

**2. Swapped roles (Running Away Balloon).** Original: Acme Carbon / Community safety
concerns / Environmental protection / Acme Carbon / Experimental CO2 storage profits. That's
five labels on a template whose roles were guessed, with the company labeled as both the
runner and the thing he reaches for. Rewrite (memegen `balloon`: the balloon twice, then
the blob pulling him away):
- "A safe lake" / "A safe lake" / "Carbon dioxide wells under it"

**3. Needs background (Surprised Pikachu).** Original: "AAF doesn't acknowledge Pelican
Bayou's existence in permit applications / Community shows up in force to oppose the
project". The setup is a permit-filing detail (behind an acronym), and the punchline is
the community, not the harm. The Roll Safe version of the same fact works (see the
calibration table), because the loophole *is* the joke.

**4. Acronyms (CO2 wells).** Original: "Acme Carbon permit doesn't identify depth of
USDW / This seems important". Rewrite (Monkey Puppet, standalone):
- "The carbon dioxide well company when you ask how deep the drinking water is"

Other old rows leaned on a bill number ("HB4") with no explanation. Say what the law does:
"the new state law letting parishes say no".

**5. Conversion error (arsenic).** "4900% above EPA standards" is right if the source says
*above*, and it's 50 times the limit. If the source says the level is 4,900% *of* the
standard, it's 49 times. Check which before converting. Rewrite:
- "Acme Carbon when residents mention Lake Heron already has 50 times the EPA arsenic limit"
  (keep "EPA limit"; "safe level" is a different claim than the source makes)

**6. Too many boxes (Boardroom).** Five captions on a four-box template: imgflip silently
dropped the punchline. Lint now fails this; keep Boardroom to four, with the middle
bubbles ≤3 words.
