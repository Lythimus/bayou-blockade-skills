# What actually changes a decision

Comment leverage does not track how strongly the comment is felt, how long it is, or how many
people sign it. It tracks one thing: **how expensive the comment makes it for the
decision-maker to proceed unchanged.**

Agencies must consider and respond to *significant* comments, and failing to do so is grounds
for setting a decision aside as arbitrary and capricious. A comment is significant when it is
specific enough to put the agency on notice of the issue and give it a chance to address the
substance. Heat does not create a response obligation. A precise defect does.

Bodies with no formal response duty, such as a bond issuer at a TEFRA hearing, work the same
way through a different channel. A precise, checkable question in the record is one their own
counsel has to answer before proceeding.

**The tier tables live in the posture file** (`postures/permit.md`, `postures/rulemaking.md`,
`postures/financing.md`). The pattern is the same in every posture:
- **Tier 1** creates legal exposure for the decision-maker itself, so fixing it is cheaper than
  defending it. It leads the letter.
- **Tier 2** builds the record and preserves issues for the next venue or an appeal.
- **Tier 3** moves discretion.
- **Tier 4** is cut, even when it is true.

Sort every finding into a tier with the posture file before drafting.

---

## Tier 4 in every posture: costs more than it earns

Each posture file may add to this list.

- **Anything outside this decision-maker's authority.** Property values and traffic in an air
  permit comment. A permit dispute put to a bond board *as if the board should resolve it*.
  Raise these with the body that has jurisdiction, or translate them into this body's terms.
- **General opposition with no record citation.** It counts as volume, not substance.
- **Emotional characterization that invites the NIMBY filing.** Assassination, poisoning,
  colonization. The underlying point survives without the phrasing. The phrasing is what lets a
  reader classify the whole document as emotional and answer it with boilerplate.
- **Any factual claim you cannot source**, including a plausible number from a snippet that
  never resolved to a primary document. One unsourceable claim discredits the sourced claims
  around it.
- **Mischaracterized case law or a statute cited for the wrong proposition.** This is worse than
  no citation, because opposing counsel reads these filings. A statute from another posture's
  toolkit counts, such as an LDEQ appeal statute cited in a bond or rulemaking comment.
- **Attribution you have not disambiguated.** Keep out a penalty that might belong to a
  differently named corporate affiliate until the attribution is resolved.
- **Accusations about the decision-maker's motives**, such as fee income or capture. They harden
  the reader and add no checkable defect.

## Structural multipliers

Whatever the content, these raise the response burden:

1. **Number the requests.** A numbered request forces an itemized answer and makes a skipped one
   visible.
2. **Ask explicitly for an itemized response**, or, where there is no response duty, for the
   letter to be transmitted to the next venue.
3. **Separate record facts from outside facts** visibly. It signals the whole document was
   checked and denies the reader the easy "unsupported assertions" dismissal.
4. **Include a candor section.** Stating unresolved items as open questions preserves the issues
   without asserting unverified facts, and shows that everything else was verified.
5. **Request written notice of the decision**, and of whatever event starts the posture's clock.
6. **File before the deadline.** A late comment is not in the record, and nothing else on this
   page matters if that happens.
