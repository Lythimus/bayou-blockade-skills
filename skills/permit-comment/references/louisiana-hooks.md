# Louisiana legal hooks — status and traps

Check every authority here before citing it. Each entry carries its current status and the
specific way it can be misused. **Never characterize a case from memory** — one case in this
file reads as favorable and is not, and it has already been miscited in a filed comment.

Add new authorities here as they are verified, with status and date.

## Constitutional and public-trust

### La. Const. art. IX, § 1 — the public trust duty
**Status: live, foundational.** Natural resources "shall be protected, conserved, and
replenished insofar as possible, consistent with the health, safety, and welfare of the
people." The source of the whole IT-factors framework. Safe to cite directly.

### *Save Ourselves, Inc. v. Louisiana Environmental Control Commission*, 452 So.2d 1152 (La. 1984)
**Status: live, controlling.** Establishes the agency's public-trustee obligation and the
"IT factors" — before issuing, the agency must determine:

1. whether potential and real adverse environmental effects have been **avoided to the maximum
   extent possible**;
2. whether a **cost-benefit analysis** shows social and economic benefits outweigh
   environmental costs;
3. whether **alternative projects, alternative sites, or mitigating measures** offering more
   protection were considered.

**Where it bites hardest:** factor 3. A record with zero alternatives analysis fails it
factually, with no legal argument required. Factor 2 is nearly as good where the only stated
justification is the applicant's own administrative convenience and no public benefit is
asserted anywhere.

### *RISE St. James v. LDEQ* (Formosa) — ⚠️ **READ THIS BEFORE CITING**

**Do not describe this as a ruling against Formosa. It reinstated Formosa's permits.**

- **19th JDC, September 14, 2022** — vacated all of Formosa's air permits on cumulative-impact
  and environmental-justice grounds. Widely reported as a landmark.
- **La. 1st Cir., January 19, 2024 — REVERSED IN ITS ENTIRETY.** The panel majority reinstated
  every permit. This is the operative decision.

**What you can accurately cite from the First Circuit opinion:** it held the *Save Ourselves*
directives "broad enough to include an analysis of environmental justice" (Op. at 46) — so EJ
analysis being part of the Louisiana public trust duty is First Circuit law.

**The trap, and it is a real one:** the same opinion endorses the sequence where, **upon
finding no adverse impact, the agency need not analyze disparate impact.** That is the
applicant's standard defense, and this case supplies it. Citing the case for EJ without
addressing this hands the agency its own answer.

**How to use it correctly:** cite it for the EJ holding, then **distinguish it on the
predicate.** The escape hatch requires a defensible no-adverse-impact finding first. Where the
record's impact finding is conclusory — one sentence, no receptor, no concentration, no
location, or made over an unresolved internal technical objection — the agency cannot take the
benefit of a sequence whose first step it never completed. Say that explicitly.

## Statutory

### La. R.S. 30:2018 — Environmental Assessment Statement
- **§ 2018(E)(2)** exempts "an application for a minor source of air emissions" from § 2018
  outright. **Verified against primary text at `legis.la.gov`.** For a minor source, the
  absence of a formal EAS from the record is **lawful**. Do not argue an EAS was required —
  that argument is unavailable and making it costs credibility on everything else.
- **§ 2018(H)** is the savings clause and names *Save Ourselves* explicitly: nothing in the
  section relieves applicants or the department of the Article IX public-trustee requirements.

**The move this supports** — used successfully in the EMPCo filing: concede the exemption
plainly, then assert the constitutional duty through (H). The statutory exemption removes the
filing formality and leaves the constitutional obligation fully intact. Conceding the point
that cuts against you buys the rest.

### La. R.S. 30:2050.21 — appeal by an aggrieved person
**The standing provision.** An aggrieved person may appeal a final permit action **only to the
19th Judicial District Court**, by petition filed **within 30 days** after notice of the action
is given.

Consequences for drafting: state standing in the letter; request written notice of the final
decision so the 30-day clock starts cleanly; do not let the comment period close without every
colorable issue raised, because unraised issues are generally waived.

### La. R.S. 30:2024 — do not confuse with the above
Pertains **exclusively to the permit applicant's** own request for a hearing within 30 days.
It has no bearing on the appeal window available to an aggrieved member of the public, which
runs under § 2050.21 from when notice of the action was given.

## Regulatory (LAC Title 33)

- **LAC 33:I.Chapter 18 — expedited permitting.** Where a large or complex application was run
  on the expedited track, ask for the designation to be rescinded and the application processed
  on the standard track commensurate with its scale.
- **LAC 33:III.531.A.1 — state-only public notice.** Watch for a notice claiming state-only
  status over a permit containing conditions marked "(Federally Enforceable)." That mismatch is
  a notice defect.
- **LAC 33:III.319 — Regulatory Permit for Flaring of Materials Other than Natural Gas.**
  Requires, per event and in advance: physical location (§ 319.G.1.e), dates and expected
  duration (§ 319.G.1.f), estimated criteria and toxic emissions (§ 319.G.1.g), separate
  notification per event (§ 319.G.3); authorization lapses 60 days after completeness
  (§ 319.D); events over 10 days need separate approval (§ 319.E); volume monitored by flow
  meter with an actual-emissions report within 30 days (§ 319.H). **No exit-velocity limit
  exists in § 319** — verified against primary text; do not assert one. In force unchanged
  since 2018.
- **LAC 33:III.513 — relocation and temporary sources.** § 513.C.1 authorizes relocation only
  for a facility "presently operating under a certificate of approval"; § 513.C.2 makes the
  test comparative against an approved baseline (plot plan, property-line distances, prior
  dispersion estimate). § 513.B.3.b (Temporary Sources) requires notice **at least 10 days in
  advance of each change in location.** Where a permit cites § 513.C with no baseline anywhere
  in the record, both readings are adverse — say so.
- **LAC 33:VII.709.5 — solid waste land use.** Requires a description of total existing land
  use within 3 miles. A missing one goes to technical completeness.

## Case law — other

### *Matter of Browning-Ferris Industries Petit Bois Landfill*, 657 So.2d 633, 637 (La. App. 1 Cir. 1992)
⚠️ **Status: CITATION UNVERIFIED — do not cite until confirmed (flagged 2026-08-29, HPLS campaign).**
The proposition attributed to it — that an alternative-site analysis performed to justify the site
the applicant already owns does not satisfy the IT factor — is the right argument and is directly on
point whenever an EAS screens alternatives by criteria only the chosen site can meet. **But the
citation itself does not check out.** The reporter volume and year are internally inconsistent
(657 So.2d is a 1995 volume; the entry gives a 1992 opinion date), and a web search on the case
name, the reporter cite, and "Petit Bois" together returned nothing matching. It was dropped from
the HPLS comment letter rather than filed on an unconfirmed cite.

**In the meantime, the argument does not need it:** *Save Ourselves* IT factor 3 supports it
directly, on the face of a record with no comparative alternatives data. Whoever next needs this
should pull the opinion from Westlaw, Lexis, or the La. 1st Circuit's own archive, fix the
citation, and restore the status line — or delete the entry.

## Federal, as applied through state permits

- **40 C.F.R. § 60.18** — flare requirements. § 60.18(c)(3) net heating value, **§ 60.18(c)(4)
  exit velocity.** The two work together: a 300 Btu/scf heat content licenses a maximum
  velocity on the order of 60 ft/sec. A permit imposing the heat-content criterion while
  omitting the velocity criterion has taken half the federal test.
- **The duty to respond to significant comments.** An agency must consider and respond to
  significant comments; failure is grounds to set a decision aside as arbitrary and capricious.
  The specificity requirement exists to confirm the agency had notice of the challenge and a
  chance to address its substance. This is the doctrinal reason specificity outperforms volume.
- **40 C.F.R. Part 60 Subpart A (§ 60.11)** — visible emissions and opacity, applicable to
  flares; relevant where dark or opaque smoke was observed and documented.
