# Posture: public financing approval

A conduit issuer or public body approving public financing for a private project:
- a TEFRA hearing under IRC § 147(f) for tax-exempt private activity bonds (LPFA, a parish
  Industrial Development Board, LCDA, a port);
- the State Bond Commission's approval of those bonds;
- related incentive approvals, such as a PILOT lease-back or a cooperative endeavor agreement.

The body is not a regulator, and nothing it decides is a permit.

## Audience: underwriters, not regulators

LPFA's board is seven gubernatorial appointees, most from commercial lending, public
accounting, real estate, and corporate law. Check the issuer's current board page before
characterizing any specific board. They evaluate a project the way a lender does. Their
vocabulary:
- contingent liability;
- event of default;
- reputational risk to the issuer in the bond market;
- Bond Counsel's opinion;
- IRS audit exposure;
- verifiable benefit to the state.

**How bonds actually fail.** Conduit issuers rarely deny a bond on the record. Bonds fail
because:
1. **Bond Counsel will not give an unqualified approving opinion.** For example, the
   exempt-facility classification is doubtful, or the obligor's identity is unclear.
2. **The bonds cannot be marketed.**
3. **The State Bond Commission defers or declines.** It is an elected, political body, and
   R.S. 9:2347(H) requires its approval for every public-trust bond issue.

A letter works by putting questions in the record that Bond Counsel must answer before it can
opine, and by building the record the SBC will see.

**Translate every environmental finding into financed-asset risk, or cut it.**
- An unresolved contradiction in the permit record is schedule and revenue risk to the asset
  being financed.
- A withdrawn EJ analysis is a reputational event, whatever the permit's merits turn out to be.

State plainly that you are not asking the board to resolve the permit dispute ("that is for
LDEQ"). The concession buys the rest of the letter.

## Interest (not a gate)

Section heading: **"Who I am."** Content: a Louisiana resident and taxpayer, family ties to the
host parish if the profile supports them, and **no financial interest in the transaction**.

**Never cite La. R.S. 30:2050.21 or "aggrieved person."** No such appeal attaches to a TEFRA
hearing, and citing it to a board advised by bond counsel is a visible, credibility-costing
error.

Standing matters later only for a validity contest, and La. R.S. 9:2347(H) gives that right to
"any person or persons in interest." Do not argue that standing in the letter.

## What each numbered request ends in

There is no condition to draft. Every request ends in one of these:
- **A record disclosure the board can require before voting.** Examples: an asset-by-asset
  accounting of what the proceeds finance; the scrap vs. virgin-feedstock percentage; the
  borrower's complete ownership structure; a written benefit-to-Louisiana showing; clawback or
  job-retention terms.
- **A Bond Counsel statement on the record.** Examples: which IRC § 142 category and which
  regulation the bond relies on, and the basis for including specific assets; whether the issue
  relies on the § 146(h) volume-cap exception.
- **Deferral** until the disclosures are in the record.
- **Transmittal** of the letter and the hearing record to the State Bond Commission before its
  vote, with the SBC docket number.
- **Written notice** of the board's action and of the **notice-of-sale publication date** (the
  clock; see below).

Requests go at the **top** of the letter, numbered, each pointing to the section that argues it.
The LPFA letter's "Requested relief" block is the model.

## Leverage tiers

The posture-neutral principle and the Tier 4 cut list are in `references/leverage.md`.

### Tier 1: could stop Bond Counsel's opinion

| Finding type | Why it lands |
|---|---|
| **The exempt-facility classification does not fit the project's own stated facts** | Test the project's own description against the actual Treasury regulation for the § 142 category claimed, not the notice's label. For solid-waste bonds, check each of these: the virgin-material exclusion, the mixed-input 65%-every-year rule, and the first-useful-product cut-off. A financed asset outside the category is a tax-exemption risk to bondholders. |
| **The notice cites the wrong IRC provision** | Check reflexively. § 146(a) is the volume cap, not a facility definition (see `references/federal-hooks.md`). A mis-citation is cheap to point out and forces Bond Counsel to state what it actually relies on. |
| **Volume-cap treatment is unclear** | § 142(a)(6) bonds are subject to the volume cap unless § 146(h)'s government-ownership exception applies, and that exception carries a checkable safe harbor. Ask which route applies. |
| **The borrower or obligor is ambiguous** | Name changes, multiple addresses, foreign parents disclosed only in an SEC filing. Bond Counsel cannot opine on an unidentified obligor. |

### Tier 2: builds the record for the SBC and any validity contest

- **The financing stack.** Every related bond, lease-back, PILOT, and cooperative endeavor
  agreement, and which of them has or has not gone before the SBC.
- **Benefit to the state.** Ask what the borrower has filed. Do not assert that the project
  fails a test (see the *Gonzales* trap in `references/louisiana-hooks.md`).
- **Historical findings about the issuer**, such as the Legislative Auditor's 2000 LPFA audit.
  **Always frame these as a question about current practice**, never as a claim that the
  deficiency is current.
- **Comparable in-state failure precedent**, with the delivered-vs-promised numbers taken from a
  primary source (for example, the parish's audited financial report, not press).

### Tier 3: moves discretion

- A deferral request tied to the specific missing disclosures.
- Venue and schedule, such as a short gap between the hearing and the SBC vote.
- Contingent liability from the underlying permit dispute, translated into asset risk as above.

### Tier 4 for this posture (cut, in addition to the shared list)

- **Fee-income conflict-of-interest arguments** (implying the issuer approves bonds for its own
  fees). They harden the board. The fee figures in the research report were also wrong for a
  post-2023 bond.
- LDEQ-style standing, appeal, or "permit condition" language.
- Anything that reads as an environmental complaint filed in the wrong forum.
- **Trap in the research report: Bond Validation Act clock.** It gives La. R.S. 13:5121 and "30
  days from the authorizing resolution" as the clock. Both are wrong. The clock is 9:2347(G),
  running from the notice of sale.
- **Trap in the research report: volume caps.** It says exempt-facility bonds "bypass" volume
  caps. That is wrong for § 142(a)(6).

## Health-mechanism section

**No standalone section.** Health facts enter only through their financial translation, as a
contested permit or a reputational event. A mechanism paragraph addressed to a bond board reads
as the wrong forum.

## Identifiers (RE block and `\filingid`)

Issuer · bond amount and series name exactly as in the notice ("Not to Exceed $…
Exempt Facility Revenue Bonds (… Project)") · hearing date · the SBC docket number and agenda
item once published.

Use a letter format addressed to the issuer's President/CEO by name, as in the LPFA letter,
rather than a docket-style RE block. Keep `\filingid` to the bond series plus the SBC docket.
Escape the dollar sign in the `.tex` file (`\$900,000,000`), or LaTeX reads it as math mode.

## Relief and closing

The requested-relief block opens the letter; it does not close it. The closing:
- asks that the letter also inform the SBC referral;
- states the incontestability clock, citing the statute and its trigger;
- asks for written notice of the board's action and of the notice-of-sale date.

## Response owed and terminal clock

- **No formal response obligation.** IRC § 147(f) requires "a public hearing following
  reasonable public notice" and approval by the "applicable elected representative." It
  requires no response to comments. That is why the letter must be self-contained and must ask
  for transmittal to the SBC.
- **Next venue:** the State Bond Commission. R.S. 9:2347(H) requires its approval before any
  public-trust bond is issued and delivered.
- **Clock:** R.S. 9:2347(G)–(H). Public-trust bonds carrying the statutory recital become
  "incontestable for any cause whatsoever after thirty days from the date of publication of the
  notice of sale." That notice is published only after SBC approval and at least seven days
  before the sale. The **Bond Validation Act (R.S. 13:5121 et seq.)** is the procedural vehicle
  for such a suit. It sets no period of its own (§ 5122).

## Posture-specific QA

- No 2050.21, "aggrieved person," "permit condition," or "Response Summary" language.
- Every environmental fact that survives is translated into financed-asset or reputational risk,
  and the letter says the board is not being asked to resolve the permit dispute.
- The IRC and Treasury-regulation cites were checked against primary text, with the right
  subsection for each proposition. The LPFA verification pass caught a rule cited to (e) that
  lives in (d)(3)(i).
- Historical audit findings are framed as questions about current practice.
- The clock names 9:2347(G) and the notice-of-sale trigger.
- The SBC docket number is included if it is published.
